Wear resistance of graphene coatings on aluminium.

Tribometer testing of several graphene coating types on aluminium substrates, comparing coefficient of static friction and wear-track depth, with applied writeups on where graphene coatings are useful in tires and rotary blades.

Freire-Gormaly Lab, York University · Researcher · April 2024 to August 2025

01

What it was

Graphene coatings are of interest as a wear-resistant surface treatment because a thin layer can measurably change how a surface behaves under sliding contact. I tested several different graphene coating types applied to aluminium substrates on a tribometer, running a tribology test procedure that records friction and produces a wear track on the sample.

Two things came out of each run, a coefficient of static friction and a measurement of how deep the wear track cut into the coating and substrate. Comparing coating types on both measures gives a read on which coatings actually protect the surface rather than just looking different under the microscope.

02

What I did

  • Ran tribometer wear tests on multiple graphene coating types on aluminium
  • Measured coefficient of static friction for each coating
  • Measured wear-track depth to compare coating durability under sliding contact
  • Processed the raw tribometer output through a tribology analysis script
  • Wrote up applied cases for graphene coatings on rubber tires and rotary blades, arguing why the same wear and friction behaviour is useful in those applications

Coating types, friction and wear-depth data, and the tire/rotary-blade writeups to be added. The tribology processing script currently lives on lab software and needs to be retrieved.
