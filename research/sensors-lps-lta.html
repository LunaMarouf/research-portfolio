Electrochemical Sensor for LPS and LTA Bacterial Endotoxin Detection.

A three-electrode straight-channel graphene device with a PEDOT:PSS working electrode, targeting lipopolysaccharide and lipoteichoic acid, the marker molecules that flag Gram-negative and Gram-positive bacterial contamination.

ACuTE, York University · Biosensor project lead · From April 2025

01

What It Is

LPS and LTA are structural molecules on the outer membranes of Gram-negative and Gram-positive bacteria respectively. Detecting them directly, rather than culturing the organism, is a faster route to flagging bacterial contamination. This device is the third sensor platform built in the ACuTE lab on the same core idea as the cytochrome c and Envelope 2 devices: a disposable three-electrode chip with a sprayed graphene base architecture.

The working electrode differs from the other two platforms. It is built on PEDOT:PSS, a conductive polymer, rather than bare graphene, which changes the surface the sensing chemistry is grown on. The straight-channel electrode layout is shared with the cytochrome c device.
