Heat Exchanger and Optimal Fin Design.

Two Heat Transfer course assignments, a heat exchanger design and an optimal fin design, both worked through in Microsoft Excel and written up as reports.

Heat Transfer, York University · Course assignments

01

What It Was

Two separate design assignments from the same course. The first was a heat exchanger design, sizing and specifying the exchanger against a given duty. The second was an optimal fin design problem, finding the fin geometry that best met the heat-dissipation requirement.

Both were analytical, worked entirely in Microsoft Excel rather than CAD, and each was written up as a design report.

02

Heat Exchanger: Oil Cooler, Double-Pipe

51 valid designs swept, cheapest one that meets every constraint wins

A double-pipe counter-flow exchanger cooling 0.1 kg/s of oil from 150°C to 50°C using 0.265 kg/s of seawater at 10°C, built from Schedule 40 pipe in 6 ft sections. Rather than hand-picking a pipe size, I swept every valid NPS pair and pass count against the pressure-drop limit (60 psi per side) and picked the minimum-cost design that still hit the required outlet temperature.

Optimal Fin Design

Pin fins beat square fins on a mass basis, not a heat-output basis

A three-part comparison: fin geometry (square vs. pin) across a range of sizes, then fin spacing at fixed geometry, then a head-to-head between the best square and best pin design on heat dissipated per gram of material.

DesignFin size / spacingq totalMassq/m
3-B, square array6 mm, 2 mm spacing111.8 W369.1 g0.311 W/g
9-B, pin array6 mm, 2 mm spacing111.8 W289.9 g0.386 W/g

The pin-fin array matches the square array's heat dissipation at 21% less mass, a 24% better specific performance. Fin spacing was not monotonic for the pin geometry either, 2 mm spacing outperformed both tighter and wider spacing in the sweep. Design report to be added.
