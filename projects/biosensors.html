Molecularly imprinted electrochemical sensors for protein detection.

Disposable three-electrode chips that detect a target protein by the shape of the cavity it leaves behind. I design them, fabricate them in batches, run the electrochemistry, and wrote the Python that turns raw instrument files into auditable results.

02

The problem

Detecting a specific protein normally means antibodies, which are expensive, need cold storage, and go off. A molecularly imprinted polymer replaces the antibody with a plastic film that has been grown around the target protein and then emptied, leaving cavities shaped like it. The film is cheap, stable at room temperature, and made on the electrode itself.

When the protein comes back it slots into those cavities and blocks the electrical signal by a measurable amount. Less signal means more protein. The readout is signal-off, which means the whole measurement depends on first proving that a film exists and that its presence suppresses current. That proof is section 05.

Two device generations exist and they are at different stages. The straight-channel device detects cytochrome c, a marker associated with liver damage and hepatitis C, and works end to end with a measured concentration response. The circular device targets the hepatitis C virus Envelope 2 protein and is at fabrication and baseline characterisation. Nothing on this page reports an Envelope 2 result.

03

Straight-channel device, fabrication methodology

Four layers, three electrodes, sprayed through stencils

A PMMA top layer, two pressure-sensitive adhesive layers acting as spacer and mask, and a PET base. Working and counter electrodes are sprayed graphene at 750 µm; the pseudo-reference is sprayed Ag/AgCl at 350 µm.

04

Molecular imprinting, straight-channel device

Grow the film around the protein, then take the protein out

Eight steps from a bare graphene surface to a film full of cytochrome c shaped cavities ready to rebind. The polymer layer is inferred from deposition charge at roughly 2 to 5 nm, thinner than the template, so the cavities are surface indentations rather than enclosing pockets.

01

APTES silanization. Amine groups laid down on graphene.

02

DTSSP cross-linking. NHS-ester linker bridges to the amines.

03

Cyt c immobilisation. Template bound through amide bonds.

04

PmPD growth. Film grown around the template, charge-limited.

05

DTT elution. Disulfide cleaved to release the template.

06

Acetic acid wash. Residual template stirred out of the film.

07

Cavities formed. Open sites matching the protein shape.

08

Rebinding. Known concentration dropcast onto the film.

Film growth is charge-controlled by chronoamperometry rather than timed, at 0.35 V in 20 mM m-phenylenediamine, limited at 141.1 µC. The 2.5 mC/cm² setpoint was chosen from a Taguchi study and is what produced the 83% reduction in oxidation-peak variance.

05

Film formation characterisation

Proving the film is there before trusting anything it does

The readout is signal-off, so the results in section 06 only mean something if a film exists and if its presence suppresses current. Three lines of evidence, one morphological, one elemental, one electrochemical.

06

Sensor response and cytochrome c rebinding

Elution opens the film, rebinding closes it

Measured against a ferri/ferrocyanide probe in 0.1 M KCl, triplicate-averaged, at 100 mV/s over a −1.0 to +1.0 V window.

The rebound trace falls below the pre-elution state rather than returning to it. That is consistent with additional non-specific adsorption at the highest concentration and has not yet been separated from cavity rebinding. Quantifying an imprinting factor requires running the non-imprinted film through the same rebinding series, which has not been done.

07

Circular-channel device, Envelope 2 protein detection

A second-generation device, designed rather than improvised

The successor to the straight-channel platform, targeting the hepatitis C virus Envelope 2 protein. A 3 mm circular working electrode for uniform current distribution, an oversized counter electrode so current is never limited there, and a closely spaced reference to minimise iR drop. Status is fabrication and baseline characterisation. No Envelope 2 concentration response has been measured.

08

Quality assurance, examples of rejected devices

Deciding which chips are worth testing

Electrochemical testing is slow and reagents are finite, so every device is inspected under the microscope first. Three rejection triggers are applied, one example of each shown below. Ag/AgCl contamination on the working electrode, reference-electrode breakage or non-uniformity, and conductive contact between the working and reference electrodes. An accepted device is shown in section 09.

Screening is an aid, not a validated defect classifier. Devices it keeps still go through dry isolation and continuity checks, open circuit potential stability, and full electrochemical validation before any result is trusted.

The curve and the device it came from

Every number is traceable to an inspected device

The screening gate only means something if the data that gets published comes from devices that passed it. These three belong together. An approved device, the voltammogram it produced, and the process record of how its film was grown.

10

Software

The analysis is code, not spreadsheets

Both tools are manifest driven. Raw instrument files are never renamed or edited; a spreadsheet maps each file to its device, stage, replicate and concentration, and that manifest is the record of what was run. Two people running the same manifest get the same answer.

Cyclic voltammetry pipeline

CV/CV_Main_Script.py

Extracts the third scan from each of three technical runs, verifies the potential axes match row by row before averaging rather than silently misaligning them, then computes point-by-point mean, standard deviation and standard error.

  • Custom peak finder with branch splitting, prominence scoring and refinement back to the raw extreme
  • Dose response fitted against linear, log-linear and power models with R² for each
  • Multi-sheet Excel workbook plus 600 dpi PNG, PDF and SVG

Microscopy screening

Microscopy/microscopy_screening.py

Groups images by device, applies the three hard rejection rules, then compares complete sets against a locally calibrated reference model. Hand-written computer vision, with contrast-normalised edge maps, FFT phase-correlation registration, and a distance-weighted three-nearest-neighbour vote.

  • Accept, conditional, hold, review and reject dispositions with reason codes
  • Out-of-distribution guard returns REVIEW rather than guessing
  • Manual override column preserved across reruns

11

Limitations and next steps

What this platform has not shown yet

Not established

  • No limit of detection or limit of quantification
  • No quantified imprinting factor or selectivity against the non-imprinted control
  • No real-sample or spiked-matrix validation
  • No direct film thickness measurement; the 2 to 5 nm figure is inferred from deposition charge
  • No stability, shelf-life or reusability data
  • No Envelope 2 concentration response on the circular device

The experiments that would close these

  • Run the non-imprinted film through the identical rebinding series to give an imprinting factor
  • Extend the concentration series below 1.5 µM to establish detection limit and response shape
  • Separate cavity rebinding from non-specific adsorption at the top of the range
  • Track charge transfer resistance across the four imprinting conditions as an orthogonal confirmation
  • Report device-to-device distribution across the batch rather than representative devices

Stated deliberately. This is a validated fabrication and characterisation platform with a demonstrated concentration response, not a diagnostic device.
